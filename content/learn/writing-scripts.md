# Write scripts that sound natural

The voice reads exactly what you write. These habits make the biggest difference.

## Use punctuation for rhythm
- **Full stop (.) or । (Hindi purna viram):** a clear pause, about a quarter of a second.
- **Comma (,):** a short breath.
- **New paragraph (empty line):** a longer pause, about 0.6 seconds. Use it between ideas or scenes.
- **Question mark (?)** and **exclamation (!)** change the tone at the end of a sentence.
- Avoid long sentences. Two short sentences sound better than one long one.

## Write numbers the way you want them spoken
| You want to hear | Write |
|---|---|
| "twenty twenty-six" | 2026 or "twenty twenty-six" |
| "₹499" spoken in Hindi | "चार सौ निन्यानवे रुपये" |
| "10 a.m." | "10 am" or "ten in the morning" |
| "50%" | "50 percent" / "पचास प्रतिशत" |
| a phone number digit by digit | "9 8 7 6 5…" with spaces |

If a number sounds wrong, write it in words.

## Abbreviations and brand names
- Write **A I** with a space if you want letters spelled out, or "AI" if the voice already reads it well. Test both.
- For tricky brand names, write them the way they sound: "AiWazz" → "Ai Wazz" or "ए आई वाज़".
- Remove emojis, hashtags and URLs; they are skipped or read oddly.

## Keep each generation focused
- Up to **5,000 characters** per generation (about 5 to 6 minutes of speech).
- For long videos, generate scene by scene. It's faster to fix one scene than to redo everything.
- Use the same voice and speed for every part of one video so it sounds consistent.

## Test and adjust
Generate a short test of the hardest sentence first. If a word is mispronounced, try:
1. a different spelling (phonetic),
2. Devanagari for Hindi words,
3. adding commas around it,
4. a different voice.
